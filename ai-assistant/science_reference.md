
# Verified Moon and NASA Knowledge

## 1. Water Ice on the Moon

NASA has confirmed that water ice exists in permanently shadowed
regions near the lunar poles. This is an established finding, not
an unresolved question about whether any polar ice exists.

In 2009, NASA's LCROSS mission detected water in material ejected
from Cabeus crater near the south pole. Later analysis found that
water in some places was present as mostly pure ice crystals.

In 2018, analysis of NASA's Moon Mineralogy Mapper data identified
multiple confirmed locations of surface water ice in permanently
shadowed lunar regions. The ice is patchily distributed; at the
south pole, much of it is concentrated in craters.

These findings confirm presence, but do not establish precise,
useful quantities across the poles or demonstrate that extraction
is practical. NASA notes that the water's origins and behavior
remain subjects of research.

Water ice could potentially support future exploration
by providing water and resources for astronauts.

After extraction and purification, water can be split
through electrolysis into hydrogen and oxygen.

Hydrogen and oxygen can be used as rocket propellants.
They do not directly form hydrazine (N2H4).

## 2. Why the Lunar South Pole Is Important

The Moon's south pole has regions that receive
extended sunlight and deep craters that remain
in permanent shadow.

The Sun stays close to the horizon, causing long
shadows and unusual illumination conditions.

Multiple permanently shadowed regions have confirmed surface water ice.
Its detailed distribution and accessible quantities remain under study.

## 3. Lunar Atmosphere

The Moon has an extremely thin atmosphere
called an exosphere.

It is not correct to say that the Moon has
absolutely no atmosphere.

The exosphere cannot support human breathing
and provides negligible protection from radiation.

https://science.nasa.gov/moon/lunar-atmosphere/

## 4. NASA Artemis Program

Artemis is NASA's lunar exploration program.

Its objectives include scientific discovery,
developing capabilities for sustained lunar
exploration, and preparing for future missions
to Mars.

NASA's published 2026 plan lists Artemis III as a
crewed demonstration mission in low Earth orbit in
2027, testing systems for future lunar landings.
NASA identifies Artemis IV as the first crewed
Artemis lunar landing, targeted for 2028.

These are planned dates and mission objectives,
not completed events or guarantees; schedules and
plans are subject to change. Older mission reports
may describe earlier Artemis architectures.

## 5. Lunar Surface

The Moon has many impact craters, and its surface
is covered with rocky debris called regolith.

Lunar surface gravity is approximately one-sixth
of Earth's gravity.
